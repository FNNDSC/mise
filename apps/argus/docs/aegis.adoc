== Abstract

AEGIS — the *ARGUS Ergonomics & Graphical Interface Standard* — is the design language of the ARGUS surface: the visual grammar, the interface philosophy, and the behavioural canon everything on the console obeys. The name is deliberate: an aegis is a protective canon, and everything on the surface is built *under the AEGIS*. Where link:../../../docs/workspace-model.adoc[the workspace model] rules on where logic lives in the mise stack, AEGIS rules on what the surface *is* — what a pane may show, where its machinery hides, what links panes together, and what LCARS means when it is taken seriously. This is a *living document*, updated as the design conversation progresses.

== LCARS: what it is, and why ARGUS started there

LCARS — the *Library Computer Access and Retrieval System* — is the fictional computer interface of the Starfleet universe, designed by scenic artist Michael Okuda for _Star Trek: The Next Generation_ (1987) and elaborated across the franchise since. Its look is unmistakable: flat rectilinear panels in warm color families, swept "elbow" junctions, pill-shaped buttons, condensed uppercase type, and dense numeric captions — all butted edge to edge with no overlap, no window chrome, no cursor-centric idiom at all. The name and the visual designs are the property of CBS Studios / Paramount; this document uses "LCARS" only as a historical and technical reference to that body of work (see the precedents section for sources).

ARGUS started from LCARS for three honest reasons. First, fit: a live operator console for medical compute wants exactly what the fiction depicts — glanceable state, fixed geometry, controls that read as regions of a single instrument rather than floating windows; the structural argument is made in link:../../../docs/workspace-model.adoc[the workspace model]. Second, discipline: beneath the costume sits a real design discipline (no overlap, color as role, frame as control) that certified operator interfaces — cockpits, radiology workstations — arrived at independently, so building against it is building against proven practice. Third, candidly, resonance: the aesthetic communicates instantly what kind of instrument this wants to be. The early experiments validated the fit — a consistent UI language formed faster under LCARS constraints than the previous ChRIS UI ever achieved without them.

== Why AEGIS exists: LCARS taken seriously

Nobody has done this. Decades of LCARS artifacts exist — launchers, skins, dashboards, templates — and all of them are cosmetic: they repaint chrome while nothing underneath obeys the language. The fiction shipped an appearance, never an interaction model, so every imitator cloned the only thing there was to clone: the paint. The absence of a serious attempt is therefore evidence of nothing; there is no failed attempt on record.

Meanwhile the *discipline* underneath LCARS is already proven under other names. Fixed rectilinear partitions, no overlap, color as system role, glanceable state under operator load — that is avionics (ECAM color semantics are law), that is the radiology workstation, that is tmux culture. ARGUS joins two proven halves that were never joined: LCARS-the-costume and LCARS-the-discipline, with live PACS traffic and execution graphs underneath — the real workload every fan project lacked.

The prop DNA carries real hazards, and policing them is AEGIS's job. Okuda designed for camera distance: glanceability over density, ornament tolerated, contrast questionable, uppercase everywhere. Where the costume fights ergonomics, AEGIS sides with the operator — and says so explicitly, rule by rule, rather than inheriting the prop by mood.

== What ARGUS is, in desktop terms

ARGUS is an unnamed tiling window manager wearing load-bearing decoration. In real tiling WMs, decoration is what got exiled — the frame shrinks to a one-pixel border, negative space. LCARS inverts this: the frame is the control surface. Capsules are verbs, the gutter is a preset switcher, elbows declare structure. The titlebar never died here; it got promoted.

The second difference matters more: jurisdiction. A window manager manages *foreign* windows and its rules stop at the border. ARGUS panes are native projections of one envelope-model family, so AEGIS rules reach inside the pane — anatomy, palette roles, claim behaviour, linkage. ARGUS is closer to acme or an avionics console than to a desktop: not a manager of applications, but a single instrument with subdivisions.

== The first principle: machinery behind the frame

*At rest, a pane shows only work and state. Machinery lives behind the frame; the frame is the handle.*

"No chrome" is the wrong reading of LCARS — the vow is no *dead* chrome and no *floating* chrome; frame-as-control is canonical. But always-visible generic meta-verbs (split, zoom, close) on every pane header multiply with every pane: six panes, six identical capsule clusters. That is noise scaling linearly with success, and in-fiction consoles show no window-management buttons at all.

The resolution is the *pane drawer*: every pane carries a top LCARS border; a gesture on that border (click) glides open a hidden control surface holding the pane's machinery — layout verbs, semantic children, pane-local controls. This is the mouse-native prefix key: tmux shows zero chrome because its verbs live behind a modal gesture, and the drawer is the same structure, pointer-shaped. ARGUS already owns the motif (the console lid), and the fiction backs it — LCARS panels slide away to reveal the machinery behind.

Corollaries:

* Every pane carries the top border. The border is a wide target (Fitts-friendly) and doubles as the pane's title bar.
* Zoom and close migrate into the drawer with everything else, or the principle is hypocrisy on day one.
* Jurisdiction split: the left gutter is *global* reconfiguration (presets, "reset to givens"); the drawer is *per-pane* machinery. The two never trade verbs.
* Every drawer verb has a command-line twin (`layout ...`), under the settled symmetry: mouse path and text path converge on the same intents.
* The keyboard path is tmux-shaped: Ctrl-B is the prefix, opening the focused pane's drawer with keyboard focus on its first verb (Tab walks, Enter fires); Esc closes any open drawer before anything else claims the key. The drawer being one surface for mouse and keyboard is the point — no second, parallel shortcut map to memorize.

Costs, accepted with eyes open: hidden machinery is invisible until taught — the first session must teach the gesture (a subtle border affordance, and the operator companion). The extra click on layout verbs is the correct trade: split is rare relative to work, and optimizing rare verbs for invisibility beats optimizing them for speed.

*Status:* the drawer shipped with the first slice — stamped panes (files, DAG, viewer, empty) carry their machinery behind the frame, header capsules gone. Remaining violation: the static PACS workspace pane keeps its old chrome until it joins the instance model.

== Semantic children: what goes into a new pane

The workspace model settled the universal answer: the pane specifier is an intent — a split yields an empty pane whose prompt claims it. The drawer completes that design rather than overthrowing it: alongside the raw split verbs, a pane's drawer offers *semantic children* — parent-contextualized, pre-composed intents.

* A file browser's drawer offers "browser here" — literally `ls <this pane's root>`, pre-filled.
* It offers "content viewer" — a view intent slaved to the parent's selection, showing whatever file the parent regards.
* A DAG pane's drawer might offer "browser into selected node" — the node's `vfsPath`, pre-filled.

These are shortcuts, not a chooser: they feed the same claim pipeline the empty-pane prompt feeds, and the empty pane remains the universal fallback item in every drawer. Because the offers derive from the parent's kind and state, the list grows with envelope models — it never becomes the hand-curated menu that scales badly.

== Pane linkage: subjects, not edges

The terminal and the file browser were linked "by accident" — and the accident is instructive: they are not linked to each other at all. Both observe the same shared truth, the session cwd. No pane-to-pane edge exists, which is exactly why it works. The radiology precedent has the same skeleton: MPR viewers (axial/sagittal/coronal) never message each other — all three observe one crosshair position in volume space. Nobody is master; the *subject* is.

AEGIS elevates the accident to law:

*Panes link by subscribing to named subjects — hub-and-spoke, never pane-to-pane.*

* *Direction for free:* slave = reads the subject; master = writes it; bidirectional = both write. Uni- versus bi-directional linkage is just read versus read/write membership.
* *Scope is membership, not ancestry.* Link groups are an overlay orthogonal to the layout tree — the tree stays pure geometry (radiology sync groups, exactly). Coupling links to tree topology would be a category error. One default is right, though: a child spawned from a parent's drawer inherits the parent's subjects — that is the semantic tracing the drawer promises.
* *Lifecycle dissolves.* A master closing leaves the subject standing; slaves hold the last value. No orphan-edge cleanup, no ownership fight.

=== The addressability test (locked)

The line between session truth and view state is principled, not case-by-case:

*If a subject's value is a VFS address (or a model reference), it is session truth and belongs to brasa. If it is continuous view-space coordinates, it is view state and stays in the surface.*

This matches the doctrine verbatim: the kernel speaks names in the namespace, never pixels. A selected file has an address — brasa. A selected DAG node has an address (`vfsPath` is on the model) — brasa. A crosshair has voxel coordinates in a rendering — argus. Frequency agrees: address-valued subjects change at human click-rate; coordinate-valued subjects change at drag-rate.

=== Regard: what the operator indicates (locked)

Selection is session truth masquerading as view state, and the precedents are decisive: X11 primary selection lives in the *server* so any client can consume it without the pointing client knowing who listens; Plan 9's plumber makes "the thing the user indicated" system truth, and whole ecosystems exist only because it was promoted from day one. brasa is the server analog.

The skeletal foundation, deliberately small: one session subject in brasa named *regard* — the thing the operator currently indicates — valued as a VFS address plus model kind, riding the same push channel as prompt context, with its schema in `menu`. The slaved content viewer subscribes to regard; the terminal's `cd` already writes the sibling subject (cwd). One subject, one schema, an existing channel.

What it buys immediately: the accidental terminal↔browser link and the deliberate viewer link become the same mechanism, and any future assist layer can ask the kernel "what is the operator looking at."

=== Regard, grilled: the two-layer model (locked)

Scope is neither purely global (X11's one PRIMARY) nor purely scoped (radiology sync groups) — it is both layers, from a single write path:

* *Group layer — where things render.* Every indicating pane sits in a link group (possibly of one). A regard write lands in the writer's group. One rendering rule: the group's regard renders in the group's viewer member; absent a viewer, the writing pane renders it inline as an overlay (exit restores the pane). The lone browser overlaying its own content and the browser routing to a spawned viewer are the same mechanism — membership differs, nothing else.
* *Session layer — what the operator regards.* Every regard write, whatever its group, also flows to brasa tagged `{address, modelKind, groupId, paneId}`; the session's current regard is the latest write with provenance attached. Not a second mechanism — the same write, observed at session level. The kernel's one-answer question stays answerable without disturbing any group's viewer.

Write semantics: a subject is a *retained cell, not an event stream* (MQTT retained-message semantics). Last write wins — a "conflict" between two writers in one group is just the operator moving their attention; the provenance tag says who wrote. A late joiner immediately receives the retained value, so spawn-then-see workflows feel alive. "No value yet" is a schema-legal state rendered as a defined LCARS awaiting-state, not an error.

Lifecycle: a group dies when its last member leaves; nothing is retained. Session regard lives in brasa and survives a surface reload. Groups and carved trees do not survive reload — session-only, consistent with the carved-tree rule.

=== Watch: the pane is the subscription (locked 2026-09-02)

Regard says what the operator indicates. It does not say what a surface is *keeping open* — and those are different nouns with different lifetimes: regard is one retained cell for the session, last write wins; an open DAG pane on a running feed is an interest that lasts exactly as long as the pane, may coexist with another pane's interest in another feed, and must die with the surface that held it. Overloading regard for this breaks the moment two panes look at two feeds, or the operator regards a file while a pipeline runs. So liveness has its own subject: a *watch*.

The kernel never polls on its own — that stays law. The open pane is the subscription: on opening a feed it sends `watch` with the feed's address, on leaving it sends `unwatch`, and a detached surface releases every watch it held. While anyone watches a feed the engine samples it — one feed-row fetch, and only when the counters moved a delta of what changed — on an adaptive cadence (three seconds while the feed changes, doubling on quiet ticks to thirty), publishing the refreshed `feed.dag` model to every surface as a session-bus envelope from the `daemon` surface, off the scrollback, because it is state rather than something the operator did. A feed that settles ends its own watch: there is nothing left to keep live. The `watched` message reports the honest state — `live`, `settled`, or `stale` when the last sample failed — and the pane's bar shows it (honest-wait: an observer must know whether what they see is current). The console form is `proc watch <feed>` / `proc unwatch <feed>`; the wire pair and the verb share one implementation, so chell can ask for the same liveness argus gets.

Payload discipline: the wire carries the whole collapsed model every time; the pane diffs it against what it holds by signature-stable group id, so a tick and a manual REFRESH are the same code path and the census only re-expands a group whose count changed.

=== Rooted versus slaved children

A DAG node click showcases the composition: the click writes group regard; the drawer's semantic children are parameterized by pane kind *and current regard value* — file view of this node, runtime inspector (execution state and progression), log view, note editor. Two distinct bindings, both from the same drawer:

* *Rooted child:* consumes the regard value at spawn time, then frozen — a browser rooted at the node's data directory stays there as other nodes are clicked. Not a group member; an intent pre-composed with a snapshot.
* *Slaved child:* subscribes to group regard and follows — click across nodes and the runtime and log panes retarget live. The MPR moment for compute.

The retained cell makes both feel right: a log pane spawned after the click shows that node's logs instantly.

=== The pulse wave and the fly-in (grilled, locked)

Two DAG behaviors, both settled by grilling on 2026-08-30.

*The pulse wave* animates the graph in dependency order: a node flares when every parent — join parents included — has flared, so a topological join visibly waits for its last input. The wave is history-honest: only nodes that actually executed (terminal success or error) fire; cancelled and still-pending nodes block their subtrees, so on a running feed the wave washes up to exactly the execution frontier and stops there. True timestamp replay (and pulse spacing that encodes wall time) upgrades this when the proc-cache metric slice lands; the dependency wave needs no new data. Trigger discipline: one wave on graph load, then the rail's PULSE pill on demand — the reference pane is a status instrument, not a screensaver. The ambient miniature is the opposite case and pulses always.

*The fly-in* makes activation literal navigation: dblclick dollies the camera into the node, and on arrival a rooted browser on the node's data directory overlays the same pane — the scene pauses underneath, never torn down, so Esc reverses the dolly and the DAG is back. One Esc press exits the node (directory back-walking is the drawer's BACK verb; Esc never depends on invisible browser depth). The overlay is the DAG pane transformed, not a new pane: it keeps the pane's identity and group, so file clicks inside the node write the pane's regard and any slaved viewer follows. The session-global gesture survives in the drawer as ENTER NODE — the old visible `cd` into the node — keeping fly-in view-local and side-effect free.

=== Contextual back (grilled, locked 2026-08-31)

Esc is a contextual back in the Android sense, not a fixed shortcut: each pane owns a navigation stack, entry gestures push levels, and Esc pops exactly one. The DAG pane's stack reads `feed list → graph → node immersion → file view`; fully inside a file, successive presses walk all the way back out to the list. A file view inside a node is a level because it is visible (settled 2026-09-02, correcting the 2026-08-30 one-press exit); directory depth inside the node is not visible and stays BACK's job — Esc never walks invisible depth. Transient chrome pops before navigation — an open drawer first, then a zoom — because chrome is view state, not a place the operator went. Selecting a feed in the list ENTERS it: the full graph takes the pane, the list steps aside, Esc returns (settled 2026-08-31, correcting an earlier half-pane "preview above the list" that split the pane and satisfied nobody). Live scrubbing — the list updating a graph as the selection moves — returns only when a context split exists to receive it, per the original design walk.

The gutter preset stays a declaration: RUNS-02 always lands on the pure feed roster, dismissing any graph a previous visit left up — no ghost renders. Every gesture in this stack funnels through a named intent (`list_reset`, `nav_pop`, enter, immerse), deliberately shaped so a text command layer — a navigation macro language driving the same intents from the embedded terminal, for testing, executable help, and replay — can bolt on without touching the gesture paths. That layer is designed but not yet grilled.

=== The desktop contract (locked 2026-08-31)

The workspace is a desktop, not a document. Before this was law, the layout tree had only a width contract: panes sat in normal page flow, every scrollable interior was capped by a viewport guess (`42vh` on the DAG canvas, the feed roster, the file listing; `calc(100dvh - 22rem)` on the viewer body), and a "full-workspace preset" was full-width but content-tall — a document cosplaying as a desktop, which the operator read correctly as clipping.

The contract: the shell owns the viewport (`100dvh`, the page never scrolls); the header takes what it needs, the console drawer its declared height, and the main region flexes into the rest; every leaf fills, every pane is a flex column, and scrolling happens inside instruments — the roster, the listing, the viewer body — never on the page. And when the fixed pieces outgrow the viewport, they YIELD in a fixed order: the console drawer shrinks before the workspace does — the workspace keeps a floor (`main { min-height: 20rem }`) because panes are the point of the page. Freezing the drawer (`flex: none`) instead crushed the workspace to a sliver on any screen where header plus console exceeded the viewport — the first regression the contract shipped. Fixed viewport fractions in pane interiors are banned: an interior is either chrome (auto-sized) or work surface (flex-fill with its own scroll). The zoom special-cases (`calc` heights for the zoomed canvas) died with it; a definite-height flex chain needs no arithmetic. Precedent: this is the acme/tiling-WM discipline the ARGUS framing already claimed — the instrument has total jurisdiction over its panes, and a pane's size comes from the composition, never from its content.

=== The console face (settled 2026-08-31, third pass)

The daemon's terminal is a surface too, and AEGIS reaches it. The settled shape, after two wrong drafts, is a strict two-screen split:

*Booting* is the classic in-scroll experience, untouched: the brain prints, its lights come alive the moment credentials clear, and the boot log with its in-stage progress scrolls beneath — the operator watches the machine come up the way they always did. Frames paint as one buffered write so concurrent log lines cannot tear the art, and the pulse stops itself the moment scrolling would misplace it.

*Ready* replaces the boot screen with the console face on the alternate buffer — the same screen vim and htop own — and the face is minimal by law: the animating brain, vertically centered, one status line (`DAEMON RUNNING · SESSION ESTABLISHED`, or `ENGINE EXECUTING` while a command runs), and the dim hint `HIT ESC TO TOGGLE THE BOOT LOG`. Nothing else — no panel, no strip. Everything informational (the boot report, the addresses, the token, the attach lines) lives on the Esc side, kept verbatim: the ready handoff halts the boot pulse *without a final repaint*, because cursor arithmetic over wrapped output is what stamped the brain across the token lines twice before.

The pulse stays honest the dark-cockpit way — idle shimmer, executing quickens, attach flares — and the mechanics beneath survive from the earlier drafts: stray writes while the face is up are caged in a ring (carriage-return redraws collapse, so a spinner's thousand frames stay one line) and flushed verbatim on Esc or on a dying boot; interactive prompts suspend the face so their readline owns the terminal; off a TTY nothing happens at all.

=== The bar law (locked 2026-09-01)

Sizing and layout machinery never rides a title bar. A bar is three things and only three: *identity* (the title), *state* (readouts like MODE — annunciation, not actuation), and *the handle* (the whole bar opens the drawer, where the machinery lives). This is machinery-behind-the-frame applied without exception: the console, which had accreted a zoom square and a close pill on its bar, reaches parity through its own locked idiom — the lid toggles, the drag strip resizes, and the language (`console zoom`, `console height <px>`) carries the rest. The LANG capsule lives on the lid strip: a machinery control on a machinery row.

End-caps are punctuation. In Okuda's grammar the rounded cap terminates a bar; it is frame, not control. Ours are passive and frame-colored. Red appears on a bar only when something is actually wrong — the flight-deck color law spent forward: a standing red half-pill on every pane was a standing false alarm, and the dark cockpit annunciates nothing at rest.

=== The drawer grammar (grilled, locked 2026-08-31)

The behind-pane drawer is a sentence, not a menu: *shape the tile, bind a neighbor, act on the content*. Three groups, three colors, identical on every pane at every depth — a newly spawned pane exposes exactly the same drawer, so composition nests indefinitely under one grammar.

*Structure* (harvestgold) acts on the pane as a tile: four placement pills — the new pane opens LEFT, RIGHT, ABOVE, or BELOW — plus ZOOM (offered only while unzoomed; Esc is the way back) and CLOSE. *Binding* (butter) is a radio, not a set of actions: UNLINKED (the unmarked default), LINKED FS (a browser following this pane's regard at the directory level — an indicated file shows its directory, an indicated node its data space), LINKED VIEWER (a renderer for whatever this pane indicates, by content type). SPLIT is the one verb that creates a pane; the selected binding says what the created pane IS. *Context verbs* (orange; red for destructive) act on the content, per pane kind, starting lean: files carries HOME, BACK, DOWNLOAD, DELETE; the DAG carries ENTER NODE and BACK. Destructive verbs run as visible console commands — a DELETE is an `rm` in the transcript, auditable, never a silent mutation behind a pill.

This completes the control taxonomy: the mode frame holds display-content modes, the drawer's structure and binding groups hold composition, and its verb group holds content actions. The old VIEWER and BROWSE NODE verbs retired into the binding radio.

=== The mode frame (locked 2026-09-03)

Display-content modes — which layout, which projection, shape or census, rows or cards or previews — are meanings the operator flips while looking, and their capsules belong on the frame, not over the field: in Okuda's grammar nothing floats over the black field, and a pill riding on the graph is a web-map control in orange. But a standing frame column costs a capsule's width from every pane, one pill or six, and the drawer alone hides state and charges a gesture per flip. So the mode frame borrows the header's own law: *a frame that gets out of the way leaves a strip.* The field is framed at rest the LCARS way — a rule along its top turning through an elbow into a spine down its right edge, solid, in the frame's color (the files pane's rule is its path header's, made sticky so it never scrolls away). Touch the spine and it widens into the elbow's vertical bar, whose segments are the mode blocks — labels on the bar, black gaps between, a plain fill to the foot: the bar is the frame, there is no panel, and it costs field width, which is the incentive to put it away. Touch the field, or press Esc, and they retract. FILTER is one of the blocks on a roster's frame — filtering is a mode — and the strip it unfolds lives under the rule.

Four rules keep it honest. Open on *touch*, never on hover: a hover only lights the strip (a beckon), because every affordance is by touch and a hover-open would fire under every orbit and drag on the DAG. The open frame *overlays* the field as a transient like a drawer — it never reflows the render buffer — and it stands in the Esc chain beside the drawers. It rides the *same edge* on every pane that has modes, and a pane with no modes wears no strip (an empty frame behind a strip would lie). And the *bar annunciates* every non-default mode while the frame is away (`MOLECULE · 2D · CENSUS`, `CARDS`), nothing at rest — dark cockpit — so state never hides behind retracted chrome. The language is untouched: a verb lowers to the same capsule whether the frame is in or out.

=== The argus language (grilled, locked 2026-08-31)

Every gesture is a sentence. Subject-first verbs — `pane split right`, `pane bind viewer`, `view runs`, `dag projection 2d`, `file delete`, bare `back` — run client-side off a reserved-subject set and never touch the wire; any other line belongs to the session. Verbs act on the focused pane by default, `@id` names a pane explicitly, and `%n` names the n-th pane created during a replay. Fidelity is by construction, not convention: a verb clicks the same DOM control the mouse would — the drawer's pills, the rail's capsules, the gutter's givens — so sentence and gesture cannot drift apart.

The command line is prefix-`:` in the tmux tradition (double Ctrl-B works too): a floating single line with Tab completion over the verb table, Esc atop the contextual-back stack, results echoed into the console transcript. A non-argus line typed there falls through to the session, so one command line serves both registers. The console itself also speaks the language — scripts, transcripts, and executable help all live in text. The LANG capsule on the console bar is the mouse handle; the reserved gutter domains stay unburned.

A DESKTOP is a saved layout described in this language: a verb script, serialized from the live tree — shape, kinds, bindings, display modes — with `%n` ordinals and no concrete addresses, per the schema law; the regard supplies addresses at apply time. `desktop save|load|show|list|delete`. Storage is dual and honest: localStorage always, written through to `~/.config/argus/desktops/` on CFS via `config write` (the durable layer's write path), and a save against a daemon that predates the builtin says so instead of pretending.

`argus verbs` prints the living verb table.

== The durable layer: config and named schemas in CFS

Per-user preference and captured layouts live as files in the operator's CUBE home — the Linux dotfile tradition, on CFS (durable CUBE file storage, not the synthesized VFS overlays). `~/.config/chell/` holds prompt choice; `~/.config/argus/` holds color scheme and layout schemas. The kernel stores bytes and never interprets them; each surface parses only its own directory. This concretizes the workspace-model dumb-store principle with zero new kernel machinery.

A *named layout schema* is a captured pane arrangement — but the capture is a template parameterized by regard, never a materialized layout: tree shape, pane kinds, and binding modes (slaved/rooted), with no concrete addresses. Addresses flow in at apply time from the regard that triggers it. The old ChRIS UI's fixed four-pane feed view (DAG, runtime, browser, context — all slaved to the current node) becomes one user-editable schema file, offered in the drawer alongside semantic children when a node is clicked. Schema files are written in the LayoutNode vocabulary already shelved in `menu`.

Terminology guard: gutter presets remain *givens* (system-owned, reset semantics); named captures are *user schemas* (operator-owned, durable). The strata, bottom to top: retained cell (live) → session regard (session) → named schema (durable).

`~/gathers` is flagged as a further durable-CFS concept central to future operation; its definition is owed and deliberately not invented here.

== Longevity: the clean-room strategy, and PHAROS

Building a serious instrument on another studio's trade dress has a shelf life, and the risk is worth naming precisely. (What follows is an engineering posture, not legal advice; institutional counsel reviews before any of it is relied on.)

What CBS/Paramount can plausibly reach: the word "LCARS" itself (an asserted trademark — historical reference in documentation is commentary; product naming is exposure); and the signature ornament — the swept elbow silhouette, the salmon/orange/lavender palette families, the Okudagram numeric-caption idiom, reproductions of actual screens. What they cannot reach is the discipline: _Lotus v. Borland_ held an interface's method of operation uncopyrightable, and _Apple v. Microsoft_ left GUI elements largely unprotectable under the merger and scènes à faire doctrines. Rectilinear tiling, no-overlap, frame-as-control, color-as-role, panels-as-verbs — functional paradigm, free to all, and independently practiced by avionics long before the fiction. The sharper near-term risk is not litigation at all but adoption: an institutional deployment wearing recognizable franchise trade dress invites a compliance veto long before a studio notices.

The architecture already contains the answer: AEGIS-the-standard — the drawer, subjects, regard, the claim rule, the tiling algebra — contains zero LCARS. The costume is one theme over tokenized CSS. The strategy is therefore dual-track:

* *PHAROS, the house visual language, becomes the default.* It keeps the entire discipline and replaces the signature ornament: the chamfer (cut stone) where the elbow was, the beacon where color-wash attention was, a role-derived harbor palette (night harbor, limestone, beacon amber, sweep white, seaglass, port/starboard for direction), its own type voice. First light — a side-by-side design study against the current look, with the keep/replace verdicts — exists as a published study; the full definition will live in `pharos.adoc`.
* *LCARS demotes to an operator-supplied skin.* The existing theme-import mechanism (fetched to the operator's machine at their choice) is exactly the right legal posture: the operator themes their own console, as with any desktop; the theme never ships in the repo. This also resolves the vendoring question around TheLCARS.com assets — they never enter the tree either way.

=== PHAROS, expanded

PHAROS carries two readings, both true. The shallow read is the recursive phrase: *Presentation House for AEGIS — Roles, Ornament, Shapes*. The deep read is an inventory — six letters, the six dimensions a visual language must define, and the chapters of the future style guide:

* *P*alette — color as role, never decoration.
* *H*ierarchy — type scale, weight, and the case law (uppercase for labels only; content keeps its case).
* *A*ffordance — what reads as operable.
* *R*hythm — spacing, cadence, motion (the beacon's sweep period, the drawer glide).
* *O*rnament — the motifs: beacon, light line, tier.
* *S*hape — the grammar: chamfer, capsule, divider.

=== The naming law

The fleet has two naming registers, and each has its own recursion style. *Kitchen names* (cumin, salsa, chili, brasa, chell — the mise en place) are ingredients, and each is a *self-recursive* backronym, GNU-style, per the table in the root README (*Cumin Underpins Management Infrastructure Needs*, *BRASA Runs Abstracted Shell Actions*, ...): a closed definition of its own function, with the layering left to the architecture docs. *Harbor names* (ARGUS, AEGIS, PHAROS, CALYPSO, HARBOR) are charters, and each expands *chain-recursively* — through the layer beneath it — so the presentation stack's dependency reads straight out of the expansions:

* *PHAROS* — Presentation House for *AEGIS*: Roles, Ornament, Shapes.
* *AEGIS* — *ARGUS* Ergonomics & Graphical Interface Standard.
* *ARGUS* — the All-*Regarding* Graphical User Surface: the hundred-eyed watchman named for what it does, and "regarding" is meant literally — *regard*, the session subject, is what the surface projects.
